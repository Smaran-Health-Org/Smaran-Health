# Task 8 — First 10 Diabetes Specialists Recruitment Playbook

Version 0.1 · 6 August 2026

## Objective

Schedule ten 15-minute discovery interviews with qualified Pune/PCMC diabetes or endocrinology specialists. This is problem discovery, not a product sale, clinical study, or request for patient data.

## Qualification check before outreach

A prospect counts as qualified only when public information or a direct reply supports all of the following:

1. Practices endocrinology, diabetology, or runs a diabetes-heavy physician practice.
2. Works in Pune or PCMC in a private outpatient setting.
3. Personally influences clinic workflow or can introduce the relevant decision-maker.
4. Sees meaningful repeat/follow-up volume; preferred threshold is at least eight follow-ups per day.
5. Is willing to discuss documentation and follow-up workflow without sharing patient-identifiable information.

Owner/co-owner doctors with an independent or small clinic are the first priority. Hospital-only consultants and unclear listings remain verification leads until the criteria above are confirmed.

## Outreach sequence

1. Start with the 12 highest-priority prospects in the tracker.
2. Use one short message through one public professional channel.
3. If there is no reply, send one polite follow-up after three business days.
4. If there is still no reply after seven business days, mark **No response** and pause.
5. Never contact a personal number or email obtained from a non-public or unrelated source.
6. Record the channel, date, response, objection, and next action in the workbook.

### WhatsApp or SMS

This is a 15-minute research conversation, not a sales call, and I won’t ask for patient information. Would you be open to a short call next week?

## Scheduling confirmation

Thank you, Dr. [Surname]. I’ve reserved [date, time, timezone] for 15 minutes via [channel]. I’ll ask about your current workflow before discussing any product concept, and I won’t request patient-identifiable information. I’ll send a short reminder on the day.

## Common objections and responses

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| “Send me the product.” | “I can share context later, but I’m first trying to understand the current workflow without leading the conversation. A short call would be more useful than a pitch.” |
| “I’m too busy.” | “Understood. I can keep it to 15 minutes before clinic, during a break, or after clinic. If now is not a good time, I will not keep following up.” |
| “We already use an EMR.” | “That is useful evidence. I’d like to understand what the EMR handles well and whether any follow-up context or documentation work remains.” |
| “I don’t allow recording.” | “The discovery interview does not need to be recorded. I will take written notes and will not ask for patient details.” |
| “Is this AI diagnosis?” | “No. The product boundary is documentation and approved follow-up memory. It does not diagnose, prescribe, change medication, or replace doctor judgment.” |
| “What do I get?” | “Your feedback will shape whether this workflow is worth building. If useful, I can later invite you to review a synthetic-data prototype.” |
| “Contact my assistant.” | “Thank you. I’ll coordinate only for scheduling and will keep the request to 15 minutes.” |

## Completion evidence

The task is complete only when the tracker contains ten prospects who are both qualified and marked **Scheduled** or **Completed**, with a date and channel. A sent message, verbal interest, or an unconfirmed time does not count.

## Safety boundary

- Do not request or store patient names, phone numbers, record numbers, clinical notes, prescriptions, diagnoses, transcripts, recordings, or identifiable examples.
- Use the Task 7 interview guide for every conversation.
- Interest in an interview or prototype does not authorize real-patient use.
- This recruitment task does not introduce autonomous diagnosis, treatment recommendations, prescription changes, unapproved sharing, public audio, cross-clinic access, or durable memory from unapproved content.
